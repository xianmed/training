# Scope of Practice

A **scope of practice** refers to the clinical actions that you are allowed to take on depending on your level of training. This lesson will teach you the scopes of practice for every level of the physician and surgeon ranks.

Firstly, as medical students, __you may only perform the skills which are taught at your level and the levels below__. You are expected to know all skills within the levels below your current one, but you may refuse to do a skill at your current level (for example, if you have not gone through that lesson yet). Medical students may not diagnose, but often residents and attendings will ask for your opinion on the diagnosis of the patient. Just try your best!



As a resident, you will be allowed to diagnose and treat patients. For physicians (ie., nonsurgical residents), you may perform all clinical skills, diagnose, and treat patients. Surgeons may only do surgery with permission at the intern year, then unsupervised after that first year. Attending physicians and surgeons may perform any skill they choose, but may fall under fire for malpractice if they do something outside of their experience (e.g., if a medical physician attempts to do surgery). For attendings, the expectation is that they know their specialty extremely well and are able to consult on patients required at any time, whereas medical students are expected to be more generalists until residency. 



Nurses and paramedics will communicate their scopes of practice to you as needed, but generally, they can do IVs, bleeding control, patient assessment, and other skills. When in a stressful situation, such as a trauma case where multiple interventions need to be accomplished quickly, it is highly recommended that you hand-off what skills they can do to those auxiliary staff, leaving the more advanced skills for yourself. Again, this is covered in later lessons.
