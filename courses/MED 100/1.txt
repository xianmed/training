# Introduction to Medical School
Welcome to the Meridian Teaching Hospital's Medical School! Our curriculum is designed to form you into a top-tier physician on Maple Hospital or any other Roblox medical institution. We will employ a few techniques in order to teach you medicine as effectively as possible:

* Asynchronous Learning: this online learning platform will be your primary means of learning through the course. We encourage students to actively study using other sites besides this one, especially once in residency. The bulk of learning will be completed on-your-own.
* Live Lectures and Case Studies: professors and teaching staff will host sessions within the discord to bolster your learning.
* Sessions and Applications: you will be expected to attend live shifts at medical organizations in order to practice your live skills.

> When attending other institution's shifts, it is expected you will remain respectful and adhere to the rules of that institution. If you come across any medical conflicts simply accept that of the higher rank and ask questions to the MTH staff later.

## Progression

The medical school is composed of four "years" of training. The first three years are somewhat crammed (7-8 courses each year), while the final fourth year is more relaxed. After medical school, you will apply and matriculate to __residency__, which trains you solely in your selected specialty.

To be promoted from a year in medical school, you must first at least read through each lesson. If you believe you are competent enough on a topic, you may skip it. While there are no exams for every course in medical school, there will be a small forty-question multiple choice exam after each year. There will also be an oral exam, which may be done in game or through voice chat. You must pass the overall examinations with 75%, otherwise you will need to retake the exam after a minimum of a week. 

## Courses

Courses are listed with the traditional "section - number" format. "MED" courses are skills courses, where you will learn practical skills for medicine (e.g., history-taking, minor surgeries, IV initiation), whereas "APP" (__A__natomy, __P__hysiology, and __P__athology) courses will teach theory for medicine (the different organ systems, diseases, and how to treat them). 

Notice that you will need to retain all previous skills from each year in order to be successful in medical school. Once you graduate and go on to a specialty, much of the knowledge can be forgotten, but *especially* practical skills will be vital to master and likely memorize. The APP courses are good to know (especially for more common systems), but in reality are provided only to give a solid foundation for residency. Those aspiring for surgery should have a strong grip on anatomy.
