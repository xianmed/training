# Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)

> In order to declare a patient in cardiac arrest, you must assess their pulse for between 5-10 seconds, or 35-45 when in colder environments.
Once a patient is in cardiac arrest, their heart is no longer pumping and their lungs are no longer exchanging air (ventilating). Therefore, it is important to continue circulating the blood in order to continue oxygenating the body. Cardiac arrest outcomes are typically poor, but can be improved by minimizing interruptions and acting quickly. __Remember__, as a medical professional, this is __not your emergency__. Never panic or get overwhelmed by the adrenaline of being on a fast-moving case, always act logically and methodically. The first main intervention is of course chest compressions. Following the CAB rule, chest compressions should be 1/3 the depth of the chest or about 2 inches, at 120bpm. The ratio of chest compressions to ventilations should be 30:2 for adults (15:2 in infants with solo rescuer). Ventilations should be done by connecting a bag-valve mask (BVM) to the patient's mouth after opening the airway. Breaths should be done every 5-6 seconds for adults, every 3-5 seconds for children, or every 1-3 seconds for infants. Pulse checks should be performed every 2 minutes. 
### Automated External Defibrillator (AED)
As soon as it is available, the AED should be applied to the patient's chest and set to analyze. When the AED indicates to shock, __clear the patient__ and press the shock button. The two shockable rhythms are ventricular tachycardia (VT/VTach) and ventricular fibrillation (VF/VFib) which will be explained in-depth in future cardiology courses. Flatline (asystole) and pulseless electrical activity (PEA) are __NOT__ shockable. 
> Infant exception: for infants <1y/o, if the pulse drops below __60bpm__ with signs of poor circulation, __treat the emergency as a cardiac arrest and begin compressions__. An infant's heart has smaller chambers, and beats significantly faster than the adult heart. Therefore, for the heart to beat at such a low level, circulation is inadequate for the body's demands and will quickly decompensate.
