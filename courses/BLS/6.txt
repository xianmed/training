# Bleeding Control & C-Collar
At the BLS level, there exist three main treatments for bleeding control, direct pressure, wound packing, and tourniquet application.
* First, attempt direct pressure onto the wound using a sterile dressing or cloth.
* If blood flow does not ebb, wound packing may be used in areas where tourniquets are unavailable. Tightly pack the wound with gauze of hemostatic dressing and continue to apply pressure. Areas include the neck, shoulder, and groin.
* Use tourniquets for the extremities. Place the tourniquet 2-3 inches above the bleeding site, and tighten. Wind and secure the windlass. Never remove the tourniquet until advanced providers give direction. A second tourniquet may be applied if bleeding continues.

The cervical collar is a device used to maintain the cervical spine's stability. If the cervical spine has an injury and is put out of place, it is possible that the patient could end up paralyzed or significantly injured. The cervical collar is applied around the neck and helps somewhat with securing the spine in place. They are a classic device used in MVAs, falls, and other traumatic incidents.
