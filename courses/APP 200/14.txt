# Multiple Sclerosis and Autoimmune CNS Disease

In these diseases the immune system attacks the nervous system. Multiple sclerosis (MS) is the most common, and a group of related disorders can look similar but need different treatment.

## Multiple Sclerosis (MS)

A chronic, immune-mediated disease of the CNS with **inflammation, demyelination, and neurodegeneration.** It is a leading cause of non-traumatic disability in young adults.

### Epidemiology and Risk Factors
* Onset typically **age 20 to 40**
* **Women** are affected 2 to 3 times more often than men
* More common in **temperate latitudes** farther from the equator
* **Risk factors:** low vitamin D, **Epstein-Barr virus infection,** smoking, obesity in adolescence, and family history

### Pathophysiology
Autoreactive T and B cells cross the blood-brain barrier and attack myelin, producing **plaques** (focal lesions) scattered through white matter. **Axonal loss** causes permanent disability.

### Clinical Features
Symptoms depend on lesion location and appear over days.

* **Optic neuritis:** painful vision loss, color desaturation (often the first presentation)
* **Brainstem and cerebellar:** double vision, **internuclear ophthalmoplegia (INO),** vertigo, dysarthria, ataxia, tremor
* **Spinal cord:** weakness, spasticity, numbness, band-like sensation, **bladder and bowel dysfunction,** and **sexual dysfunction**
* **Sensory:** numbness, tingling, **Lhermitte sign** (electric shock down the spine with neck flexion)
* **Fatigue:** very common and disabling
* **Cognitive and mood changes**
* **Uhthoff phenomenon:** worsening of symptoms with heat or exercise
* **Trigeminal neuralgia in a young patient** should prompt MS evaluation

### Disease Courses
* **Clinically isolated syndrome (CIS):** a first attack suggestive of MS
* **Relapsing-remitting MS (RRMS):** about 85 percent at onset. Attacks with recovery between them.
* **Secondary progressive MS (SPMS):** progressive worsening after an initial relapsing course
* **Primary progressive MS (PPMS):** progressive from onset, about 10 to 15 percent. Later onset, equal in men and women, often spinal cord.

### Diagnosis (McDonald Criteria)
MS is diagnosed by showing damage **disseminated in space (different CNS regions) and in time (different points in time),** and by excluding other causes.

* **MRI brain and spinal cord with gadolinium:**
  * T2/FLAIR white matter lesions that are **periventricular, juxtacortical, infratentorial, or in the spinal cord**
  * **Dawson fingers:** ovoid lesions perpendicular to the ventricles
  * **Gadolinium-enhancing lesions** indicate active inflammation
* **Lumbar puncture:** **oligoclonal bands** in CSF (and not in serum), mildly raised protein
* **Evoked potentials** (occasionally)
* **Exclude mimics:** B12 deficiency, vasculitis, sarcoidosis, neurosyphilis, Lyme disease, HIV, and other demyelinating diseases (NMOSD and MOGAD).

### Treatment of Relapses
* **High-dose corticosteroids:** **IV methylprednisolone 1 g daily for 3 to 5 days** (or high-dose oral equivalent), which speeds recovery but does not change long-term outcomes
* **Plasma exchange** for severe relapses that do not respond to steroids
* Rule out infection (such as a urinary infection) as a trigger for pseudo-relapses

### Disease-Modifying Therapies (DMTs)
These reduce relapses and new MRI lesions, and slow disability progression. Early treatment is key.

* **Injectables:** **interferon beta, glatiramer acetate**
* **Oral:** **dimethyl fumarate, teriflunomide, fingolimod, siponimod, cladribine**
* **Infusions (high efficacy):** **natalizumab, ocrelizumab, ofatumumab, ublituximab, alemtuzumab**
* **Important safety issues:**
  * **Natalizumab:** risk of **progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML)** from JC virus. Test for JC antibodies.
  * **Fingolimod:** bradycardia on starting, macular edema, infections
  * **Anti-CD20 drugs:** infections, low immunoglobulins, and reduced vaccine response. Vaccinate before starting.
  * **Pregnancy planning:** counsel before conception, as DMT choice depends on this.

### Symptomatic Management
* **Spasticity:** baclofen, tizanidine, physiotherapy
* **Fatigue:** exercise, sleep, modafinil, amantadine
* **Bladder dysfunction:** anticholinergics for overactive bladder, intermittent catheterization for retention
* **Pain and paresthesias:** gabapentin, duloxetine, carbamazepine
* **Walking:** **dalfampridine** can improve walking speed
* **Depression and cognitive issues**
* **Vitamin D, exercise, smoking cessation, and vaccinations**

## Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (NMOSD)

An autoimmune astrocytopathy, most often caused by **antibodies against aquaporin-4 (AQP4).**

* **Severe optic neuritis** (often bilateral)
* **Longitudinally extensive transverse myelitis (LETM):** a spinal cord lesion spanning **3 or more vertebral segments**
* **Area postrema syndrome:** intractable hiccups, nausea, and vomiting
* More common in women and people of African and Asian ancestry
* Diagnosis: **AQP4-IgG antibody** in serum, and MRI
* **Treat relapses** with high-dose steroids and plasma exchange
* **Prevention:** rituximab, inebilizumab, satralizumab, eculizumab
* **Many MS drugs (interferon beta, natalizumab, fingolimod) can worsen NMOSD,** so accurate diagnosis matters.

## MOG Antibody-Associated Disease (MOGAD)

Antibodies against myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein. Causes **optic neuritis (often with disc swelling and bilateral),** transverse myelitis, and ADEM-like presentations, particularly in children. Often steroid-responsive. Often monophasic, but relapses occur.

## Acute Disseminated Encephalomyelitis (ADEM)

* A **monophasic** demyelinating illness, often **after an infection or vaccination,** mainly in **children**
* **Encephalopathy** (altered consciousness, behavior change) plus **multifocal neurologic deficits,** with fever and headache
* MRI: large, bilateral, poorly defined lesions
* **Treatment:** high-dose IV steroids, with IVIG or plasma exchange if there is no response
* Most children recover well

## Transverse Myelitis

Inflammation across the spinal cord causing a **sensory level, weakness, and bladder and bowel dysfunction** developing over hours to days.

* **Causes:** MS, NMOSD, MOGAD, infection (viral), systemic lupus, Sjögren syndrome, sarcoidosis, post-vaccination, and idiopathic
* **MRI** of the spine and brain, and CSF analysis
* **Treatment:** IV steroids and plasma exchange for severe cases

## Autoimmune Encephalitis

Antibodies against neuronal surface or intracellular antigens cause subacute (days to weeks) **encephalopathy.**

### Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis
* **Young women** (and children)
* **Prodromal flu-like illness,** then **psychiatric symptoms** (psychosis, agitation, insomnia), memory loss, **seizures,** decreased consciousness, **orofacial and limb dyskinesias,** autonomic instability, and hypoventilation
* **CSF:** pleocytosis, **anti-NMDAR antibodies** (most sensitive in CSF)
* **Associated with ovarian teratoma:** search with pelvic MRI or ultrasound, and remove the tumor
* **Treatment:** high-dose steroids, **IVIG or plasma exchange,** then rituximab or cyclophosphamide if no response

### Limbic Encephalitis
Subacute memory loss, confusion, seizures, and mood change, with temporal lobe changes on MRI. Antibodies include **LGI1** (associated with hyponatremia and faciobrachial dystonic seizures) and **CASPR2.** May be **paraneoplastic** (anti-Hu with small cell lung cancer, anti-Ma2 with testicular tumors).

### Paraneoplastic Syndromes
Immune responses triggered by tumors cause neurologic disease, often **before the cancer is found.** Examples: paraneoplastic cerebellar degeneration (anti-Yo, breast and ovarian), opsoclonus-myoclonus, Lambert-Eaton syndrome (small cell lung cancer), and sensory neuronopathy (anti-Hu).

## Other Inflammatory Conditions

* **Neurosarcoidosis:** cranial neuropathies (especially facial), meningitis, hypothalamic involvement
* **CNS vasculitis**
* **Neuro-Behçet disease**
* **Neuropsychiatric lupus**

## Distinguishing MS From Its Mimics

* **MS:** young adult, typical short-segment lesions, oligoclonal bands, optic neuritis with a normal-looking or mild disc
* **NMOSD:** severe optic neuritis, **long** spinal lesions, vomiting, AQP4 antibody
* **MOGAD:** disc swelling, children, MOG antibody
* **ADEM:** child, post-infection, encephalopathy, monophasic
* **Vitamin B12 deficiency and vasculitis** can mimic MS on MRI.

> **Summary**
> MS is an immune-mediated demyelinating disease of young adults, typically relapsing-remitting. It is diagnosed by MRI and CSF oligoclonal bands showing lesions disseminated in space and time. Treat relapses with IV methylprednisolone, and treat early with disease-modifying therapy, watching for PML with natalizumab. NMOSD (AQP4 antibody, long spinal lesions, severe optic neuritis) is treated differently and can be worsened by MS drugs. ADEM is a monophasic post-infectious illness in children. Anti-NMDAR encephalitis causes psychosis, seizures, and dyskinesias in young women and may be linked to an ovarian teratoma.
