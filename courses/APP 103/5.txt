# Refractive Errors and Presbyopia

Refractive errors are the most common cause of reduced vision worldwide, and the most treatable. They occur when the eye's optical system does not focus light exactly on the retina.

## The Basics

Light focuses depend on two things: the **power** of the cornea and lens, and the **length** of the eye. A mismatch causes blur. Power is measured in **diopters (D)**. Minus (-) lenses diverge light. Plus (+) lenses converge light.

> **Clinical Pearl:** A patient whose vision improves with a pinhole almost certainly has a refractive error.

## Myopia (Nearsightedness)

**Distant objects are blurry. Near objects are clear.**

### Mechanism
The eye is too long (axial myopia) or the cornea too steep, so light focuses in front of the retina.

### Features
* Usually begins in childhood or adolescence
* Increasing worldwide
* Squinting to see distant objects

### Correction
**Minus (concave) lenses.**

### Risks of High Myopia
High myopia stretches the retina and raises the risk of **retinal tears and detachment, glaucoma, cataract, and myopic macular degeneration**. Patients with high myopia should be taught the warning signs of retinal detachment.

### Slowing Progression in Children
Low-dose atropine drops (0.01%), special multifocal contact lenses, orthokeratology (overnight shaping lenses), and outdoor time all slow progression.

## Hyperopia (Farsightedness)

**Near objects are blurry. Distant objects may be clear.**

### Mechanism
The eye is too short or the lens too weak, so light would focus behind the retina.

### Features
* Young people compensate by accommodating (using their lens), so distance vision may seem normal
* Symptoms: eye strain, headaches, difficulty reading, crossing of the eyes in children (accommodative esotropia)
* As accommodation fades with age, symptoms worsen

### Correction
**Plus (convex) lenses.**

### Risk
Shallow anterior chambers in hyperopic eyes predispose to **angle closure glaucoma**.

## Astigmatism

**Blurry vision at all distances, with distortion of shapes.**

### Mechanism
The cornea (or lens) is shaped more like a football than a basketball, so it has different curvatures in different directions. Light focuses at two different points.

### Correction
**Cylindrical lenses** in glasses or **toric** contact lenses. Surgery can reshape the cornea.

## Presbyopia

**Age-related loss of near focusing.**

### Mechanism
The lens hardens and the ciliary muscle weakens, so the lens cannot round up for near work.

### Features
* Onset around age 40 to 45
* Needing to hold reading material farther away
* Worse in dim light and at the end of the day

### Correction
Reading glasses, bifocals, progressive lenses, multifocal contact lenses, or monovision (one eye set for distance and one for near).

## Anisometropia

A large difference in refractive error between the two eyes. In young children it can cause **amblyopia**, because the brain favors the clearer eye (Lesson 16).

## Methods of Correction

### Spectacles
Safe and simple. Updated prescriptions every 1 to 2 years.

### Contact Lenses
Soft, rigid gas permeable, and specialty lenses. They provide wider vision and are used in sports, but they carry risk.

#### Complications of Contact Lenses
* **Microbial keratitis** (including Pseudomonas and Acanthamoeba)
* Giant papillary conjunctivitis
* Corneal neovascularization from chronic oxygen deprivation
* Dry eye

#### Safe Use Rules
* Never sleep in lenses unless specifically designed for it
* Never use tap water, swim, or shower with lenses
* Replace the case frequently
* Remove lenses and seek care for any red, painful eye

### Refractive Surgery
* **LASIK:** a flap is created and the corneal stroma reshaped with a laser.
* **PRK:** surface laser reshaping with no flap. Longer recovery, but better for thin corneas.
* **SMILE:** a small lenticule is removed through a tiny incision.
* **Implantable lenses (ICL):** for high myopia in patients who are not good laser candidates.

#### Surgical Risks
Dry eye, glare and halos at night, under- or overcorrection, and rarely corneal ectasia (weakening and bulging). **Keratoconus** and thin corneas are contraindications.

## Related Condition: Keratoconus

A progressive thinning and cone-shaped bulging of the cornea, usually beginning in teenage years. It causes irregular astigmatism and glare. Linked to eye rubbing, atopy, and genetics. Treated with specialty contact lenses and **corneal cross-linking** to stiffen the cornea (Lesson 8).

## Key Concept: Refraction

The process of finding the correct lenses is called **refraction**. The patient views a chart and chooses between lens options ("one or two?") until the sharpest vision is found. Results are written as **sphere / cylinder / axis**, for example -2.00 -0.75 x 180.

> **Summary**
> Myopia is a long eye corrected with minus lenses. Hyperopia is a short eye corrected with plus lenses and is a risk factor for angle closure. Astigmatism is uneven corneal curvature. Presbyopia is age-related loss of accommodation beginning around age 40. High myopia raises the risk of retinal detachment. Contact lens wearers with a painful red eye must be seen the same day.
