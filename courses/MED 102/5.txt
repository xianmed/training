# Coagulation Studies

Hemostasis (stopping bleeding) depends on platelets, clotting factors, and the vessel wall. Coagulation tests measure the clotting-factor pathway, and are essential for monitoring anticoagulants, evaluating bleeding, and screening for clotting disorders.

> **Specimen:** **Light blue-top tube (sodium citrate).** The tube must be **filled to the line** (a 9:1 ratio of blood to citrate). **Underfilling** falsely prolongs clotting times. Draw it **after** blood culture bottles and **before** other additive tubes.

## The Clotting Cascade in Brief

* **Extrinsic pathway:** triggered by tissue factor. Involves **factor VII.** Measured by the **PT.**
* **Intrinsic pathway:** involves **factors XII, XI, IX, and VIII.** Measured by the **aPTT.**
* **Common pathway:** **factors X, V, II (prothrombin), and I (fibrinogen).** Measured by both.

Clot formation also requires **platelets** and **calcium.** The natural brakes include **antithrombin, protein C,** and **protein S.**

## Prothrombin Time (PT) and INR

* **PT:** normal about **11 to 13.5 seconds**
* **INR (International Normalized Ratio):** standardizes PT results across laboratories. Normal about **0.8 to 1.1.**
* **Measures:** the extrinsic and common pathways (factors VII, X, V, II, and fibrinogen)
* **Most sensitive to factor VII,** which has the shortest half-life (about 6 hours). That is why the PT rises first with vitamin K deficiency, early liver disease, and early warfarin therapy.

### Uses
* **Monitoring warfarin:**
* Typical target INR **2.0 to 3.0** (for atrial fibrillation, venous thromboembolism, and most indications)
* **2.5 to 3.5** for mechanical mitral valves and some high-risk situations
* **Assessing liver function** (synthetic capacity)
* **Evaluating bleeding or coagulopathy**
* **Detecting vitamin K deficiency**

### Causes of a Prolonged PT/INR
* **Warfarin**
* **Vitamin K deficiency** (malnutrition, fat malabsorption, long-term antibiotics, newborns)
* **Liver disease**
* **DIC**
* **Factor VII deficiency**
* **Direct oral anticoagulants** (mild effect, especially rivaroxaban)

## Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (aPTT)

* **Normal:** about **25 to 35 seconds**
* **Measures:** the intrinsic and common pathways
* **Monitors unfractionated heparin:** the therapeutic target is typically **1.5 to 2.5 times the control value** (about 60 to 80 seconds, depending on laboratory protocol)

### Causes of a Prolonged aPTT
* **Heparin** (unfractionated)
* **Hemophilia A (factor VIII deficiency) and hemophilia B (factor IX deficiency)**
* **Factor XI deficiency**
* **Von Willebrand disease** (because von Willebrand factor carries factor VIII)
* **Antiphospholipid antibodies (lupus anticoagulant):** prolonged aPTT in vitro but **thrombosis** (not bleeding) in vivo
* **Factor XII deficiency:** prolonged aPTT but **no bleeding**
* **Liver disease, DIC, vitamin K deficiency, and warfarin** (when severe)
* **Direct thrombin inhibitors** (dabigatran, argatroban, bivalirudin)

### Mixing Study
When the aPTT (or PT) is prolonged, mix the patient plasma 1:1 with normal plasma:
* **Corrects** to normal: a **factor deficiency**
* **Does not correct:** an **inhibitor** (such as lupus anticoagulant, an acquired factor VIII inhibitor, or heparin)

## Interpreting PT and aPTT Together

* **Prolonged PT only:** factor VII deficiency, early warfarin, early vitamin K deficiency, mild liver disease
* **Prolonged aPTT only:** hemophilia A or B, factor XI deficiency, von Willebrand disease, heparin, lupus anticoagulant
* **Both prolonged:** common pathway deficiency (factors X, V, II, fibrinogen), **liver disease, DIC, severe vitamin K deficiency, warfarin overdose, heparin at high levels, DOACs**
* **Both normal, but bleeding:** **platelet disorders** (low count or poor function), **von Willebrand disease** (can have normal aPTT), factor XIII deficiency, vascular disorders, mild factor deficiencies, and **fibrinolytic problems**

> **Clinical Pearl:** Normal PT and aPTT **do not rule out a bleeding disorder.** Platelet function problems and von Willebrand disease are common causes of mucosal bleeding with normal screening times.

## Other Coagulation Tests

### Thrombin Time (TT)
Tests the conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin. **Prolonged** by heparin, **direct thrombin inhibitors (dabigatran),** low or abnormal fibrinogen, and high fibrin degradation products.

### Fibrinogen
Normal about **200 to 400 mg/dL.** Low in **DIC,** liver disease, massive transfusion, and thrombolysis. **Raised** as an acute phase reactant. **Below 100 mg/dL** carries a high bleeding risk.

### D-Dimer
A breakdown product of **cross-linked fibrin.** Raised when a clot has formed and is being broken down.

* **Normal:** below about **500 ng/mL FEU.** Use an **age-adjusted cutoff** (age multiplied by 10 ng/mL for patients over 50) to improve specificity in older patients.
* **High sensitivity, poor specificity.** Used to **rule out** venous thromboembolism and pulmonary embolism **in patients with a low or intermediate pretest probability.**
* **Raised in:** DVT and PE, DIC, aortic dissection, surgery and trauma, **pregnancy,** infection, cancer, older age, and inflammation.
* **Do not use** D-dimer in patients with **high pretest probability.** Go directly to imaging.

### Anti-Factor Xa Assay
Measures the level of drug activity.
* **Low molecular weight heparin** (enoxaparin) monitoring in kidney disease, pregnancy, obesity, and extremes of weight
* **Unfractionated heparin** monitoring, which is not affected by baseline abnormalities like lupus anticoagulant
* **Factor Xa inhibitors** (apixaban, rivaroxaban) when a **drug-calibrated** assay is available

### Activated Clotting Time (ACT)
A bedside test used during **cardiac surgery, cardiac catheterization, and ECMO** to monitor high-dose heparin. Baseline about 100 to 140 seconds, targeting **300 to 400 seconds or higher** for bypass.

### Platelet Function Tests
* **PFA-100:** screens for platelet dysfunction and von Willebrand disease
* **Light transmission aggregometry:** the gold standard
* **Bleeding time:** obsolete

### Viscoelastic Tests (TEG and ROTEM)
Whole-blood tests that show the clot's **formation, strength, and breakdown** in real time. Used in **trauma, liver transplantation, cardiac surgery, and obstetric hemorrhage** to guide targeted transfusion (red cells, plasma, platelets, cryoprecipitate, fibrinogen, and antifibrinolytics).

### Specific Factor Assays
Factor VIII, IX, XI, and others, when a deficiency is suspected. **Von Willebrand testing:** vWF antigen, vWF activity (ristocetin cofactor), and factor VIII.

## Monitoring Anticoagulants

* **Warfarin:** **INR** (check regularly, after dose changes, and with new drugs)
* **Unfractionated heparin:** **aPTT** or **anti-Xa**
* **LMWH:** usually no monitoring. Anti-Xa in special populations.
* **DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban, dabigatran):** routine monitoring is **not needed.** Check kidney function. In emergencies, drug-specific assays (anti-Xa or dilute thrombin time) help. **A normal PT or aPTT does not exclude a clinically significant DOAC effect.**
* **Argatroban and bivalirudin:** aPTT or ACT

### Reversal Agents (Brief)
* **Warfarin:** **vitamin K** and **four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate (4F-PCC)**
* **Dabigatran:** **idarucizumab**
* **Apixaban and rivaroxaban:** **andexanet alfa** or **4F-PCC**
* **Unfractionated heparin:** **protamine**

## Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)

Widespread activation of clotting that consumes platelets and clotting factors, causing both **clots and bleeding.** It is always **secondary** to another condition.

* **Triggers:** sepsis, trauma, obstetric complications (placental abruption, amniotic fluid embolism), cancer (especially acute promyelocytic leukemia), and burns
* **Laboratory pattern:**
* **Low platelets**
* **Prolonged PT and aPTT**
* **Low fibrinogen**
* **High D-dimer**
* **Schistocytes** on the smear
* **Treatment:** **treat the underlying cause,** and replace blood products if bleeding

## Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT)

An immune reaction to heparin-PF4 complexes, causing **thrombocytopenia and paradoxical thrombosis.**

* **Timing:** platelets fall **5 to 10 days** after starting heparin (sooner with recent exposure). A fall of **more than 50 percent** from baseline.
* **Assess probability** using the **4Ts score** (Thrombocytopenia, Timing, Thrombosis, oTher causes).
* **Tests:** **PF4/heparin antibody ELISA** (sensitive), then the **serotonin release assay** (specific).
* **Management:** **stop all heparin immediately** (including flushes and heparin-coated catheters), and give a **non-heparin anticoagulant** (argatroban, bivalirudin, or fondaparinux). **Avoid platelet transfusions** unless life-threatening bleeding. **Do not start warfarin** until platelets recover.

## Thrombotic Microangiopathies

* **TTP:** **ADAMTS13 deficiency.** Low platelets, microangiopathic hemolysis (schistocytes), neurologic symptoms, kidney problems, and fever. **Normal PT and aPTT** (this distinguishes it from DIC). **Plasma exchange** is urgent.
* **HUS:** typically after Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli* in children, with kidney failure.

## Thrombophilia Testing

Used selectively in unexplained or recurrent clotting, thrombosis at a young age or unusual sites, or a strong family history.

* **Factor V Leiden and prothrombin G20210A** (genetic tests, can be done at any time)
* **Antithrombin, protein C, protein S levels**
* **Antiphospholipid antibodies:** lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, and anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I (repeat at least 12 weeks apart)
* **Homocysteine, JAK2, and PNH testing** in selected cases

> **Clinical Pearl:** Several of these tests are **unreliable during acute thrombosis or while on anticoagulants** (levels of antithrombin, protein C, and protein S fall, and lupus anticoagulant testing is affected by heparin and DOACs). Time testing carefully.

## Pre-Analytic Pitfalls

* **Underfilled tube,** clotted sample, or **hemolysis**
* **High hematocrit** (above 55 percent) requires adjusted citrate
* **Drawing from a heparinized line** contaminates the sample
* **Delay** in processing

## Pre-Operative Screening

Routine PT/aPTT in patients with **no bleeding history** is **not recommended.** A **structured bleeding history** (previous surgery, dental extractions, easy bruising, nosebleeds, heavy periods, family history) is a better screening tool.

> **Summary**
> The PT/INR tests the extrinsic and common pathways and monitors warfarin (target 2 to 3 in most patients). The aPTT tests the intrinsic and common pathways and monitors unfractionated heparin. Use a mixing study to tell a factor deficiency from an inhibitor. A normal PT and aPTT do not exclude platelet disorders or von Willebrand disease. D-dimer rules out clots in low-risk patients but cannot confirm them. DIC shows low platelets, prolonged times, low fibrinogen, and high D-dimer. In HIT, stop heparin and use a non-heparin anticoagulant. TTP has normal PT and aPTT.
